# The T&S Handbook

> **How do you build and run a Trust & Safety team, from the first hire to a regulated program at scale?**

A practitioner's handbook in 19 chapters, for founders, product leaders and new Trust & Safety leads. Each chapter covers one part of the job: what good looks like at your stage, how to do it, the mistakes that cost teams most, a template to start from, and the [T&S Workbench](https://stevenmacchia.com/ts-workbench/) tool that does the work with you.

**[Read it online](https://stevenmacchia.com/ts-handbook/)** · **[Steven's writing](https://stevenmacchia.com/writing/)** · free, no sign-up

> **Not legal advice.** Where this handbook describes laws, it's general information. Laws differ by country, change often and apply differently to each service, so check with your own legal team or outside counsel before acting on anything here.

**In progress.** Chapters are published as they're finished. The contents below show where each one stands.

## What this handbook believes

Every chapter is written from the same point of view. These are the principles behind it, each from something Steven has written.

1. **Measure impact, not activity.** More removals can mean better detection, more harm or more good users caught by mistake. Report outcomes next to operations, and say so when they disagree. *([Activity is easy to measure. Impact is harder.](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_if-your-team-took-down-40-more-harmful-content-share-7508565523224432642-JJno/) · [Harassed players look like your best-retained users](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-gamedev-userretention-share-7511499525035847680-wRsb/))*
2. **Automate as far as the evidence supports, and no further.** Where a wrong decision can't be reversed or someone's safety is at risk, automation prepares the case and a person closes it. *([Automation rate isn't a measure of maturity](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-contentmoderation-responsibleai-share-7507791297672486913-jc9j/))*
3. **Automation earns its scope.** It expands into a new area only when its overturn rate matches human review, and it rolls back when that rate moves. *([Appeal overturns: the early warning for automation](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_as-more-enforcement-moves-to-automation-share-7508900187977854976-DLyv/))*
4. **Harm is a pattern, not a message.** Grooming, raids and scams build over time, so the response should build as signals stack. *([Grooming is a pattern, not a message](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-childsafety-onlinesafety-share-7511067864448237568-k7h4/) · [Games are where kids socialize now, and regulators know it](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_games-have-become-one-of-the-main-places-share-7510446882154864641-n3PV/) · [Watch the rate, not the volume](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_kenzie-wilson-at-stream-published-a-super-ugcPost-7511811574127312896-HAsd/))*
5. **Put friction where the risk is.** Safer defaults and targeted limits stop most harm before anything needs removing, and most users never feel them. *([Age assurance: what should each check unlock?](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_most-of-the-debate-about-age-assurance-in-share-7510763675658477568-IVax/) · [Watch the rate, not the volume](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_kenzie-wilson-at-stream-published-a-super-ugcPost-7511811574127312896-HAsd/) · [Run the abuse pre-mortem at design review](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-safetybydesign-productmanagement-share-7511792474445750272-3rLV/))*
6. **A ban is one move, not a closed case.** Offenders come back on new accounts and move across platforms, so map the network and share signals. *([A banned account is not a closed case](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_childsafety-trustandsafety-responsibleai-share-7509260900831260674-gar3/) · [Nudify apps: harm no single platform sees in full](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_nudify-apps-are-a-growing-problem-and-a-share-7510771967986155520-s7TP/))*
7. **Age assurance is the foundation.** Every protection for children assumes you know who's a child, and each level of assurance should unlock only what it can support. *([Age assurance: what should each check unlock?](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_most-of-the-debate-about-age-assurance-in-share-7510763675658477568-IVax/) · [Saturday reading: child safety gaps in games, Ofcom in court and California's new laws](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-onlinesafety-gamingsafety-share-7512121016282882048-B4fp/))*
8. **Get in at design review, and earn the invite.** Before launch, changing a default is an edit. After launch, it's taking something away. Be selective about what gets a full review, and answer in days. *([Run the abuse pre-mortem at design review](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-safetybydesign-productmanagement-share-7511792474445750272-3rLV/) · [Launches get a security review. Almost none get an abuse review.](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_most-product-launches-get-a-security-review-share-7511154971653070848-PMG-/) · [You can't moderate your way out of a systems problem](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7506790883938295809/))*
9. **Be able to prove it works.** Regulators, courts and attorneys general have moved from "do you have a policy?" to "can you prove it works?" That takes a written record of every change and one named owner for every risk and threshold, because when two teams think they share a harm, nobody owns it. *([What I'd tell myself in year one of Trust & Safety](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-onlinesafety-contentmoderation-share-7513978054688395266-P3DE/) · [The era of voluntary child safety is ending](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-contentmoderation-onlinesafety-share-7510666169993805824-VI4v/) · [Grooming is a pattern, not a message](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-childsafety-onlinesafety-share-7511067864448237568-k7h4/) · [Age assurance: what should each check unlock?](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_most-of-the-debate-about-age-assurance-in-share-7510763675658477568-IVax/))*
10. **Safety is a retention and revenue question.** Prove it with your own data, cut correctly, so the program isn't judged as a cost center by its throughput. *([Harassed players look like your best-retained users](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_trustandsafety-gamedev-userretention-share-7511499525035847680-wRsb/) · [Activity is easy to measure. Impact is harder.](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/stevenmacchia_if-your-team-took-down-40-more-harmful-content-share-7508565523224432642-JJno/))*

## Who it's for

- A founder or product leader who has just realized safety is now part of their job
- The first safety hire, building from nothing
- A new Trust & Safety leader inheriting a team, or growing one into new markets and new laws
- Anyone moving into Trust & Safety from operations, policy, legal or data

Examples lean on gaming, social and user-generated content platforms, Steven's background, and on child safety, where the stakes are highest.

## How to read it

Read Part 1 in order. After that, go to the chapter for the problem in front of you.

Every chapter opens with a one-minute summary, then shows what good looks like at three stages. They're the same stages the [program maturity model](https://github.com/stevenmacchia/ts-maturity-model) uses:

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Early** | A founder or first safety hire covers trust and safety, usually with under a million users. |
| **Growing** | A dedicated safety team, millions of users, and new markets or features on the way. |
| **At scale or regulated** | Tens of millions of users, a heavily regulated sector, or extra duties as a very large platform under EU or UK law. |

## How it's built

The chapters are plain Markdown, so they read well here on GitHub. [`build.js`](build.js) turns them into the website at [stevenmacchia.com/ts-handbook](https://stevenmacchia.com/ts-handbook/), one page per chapter, with no dependencies. GitHub Pages serves [`docs/`](docs/). Chapter status comes from the Contents tables above, so change it there.

```bash
npm run build   # rebuilds docs/ after editing a chapter or this README
```

## Goes with T&S Workbench

The handbook explains the job. The [Workbench](https://stevenmacchia.com/ts-workbench/) gives you free, private tools to do it: an abuse pre-mortem, an incident tabletop, a metrics framework, a vendor scorecard, a coverage radar, a program maturity model, a classifier eval, compliance readiness, and AI assistants for policy, notices and appeals. Each chapter links the tools that go with it.
